5Emotion vectors predict and move the model's preferences

We showed the model two activities at a time and asked which it would rather do, over every ordered pair. Fitting those answers together gives one ranking of what the model prefers. The paper tests its emotion vectors against such a ranking in two ways: by reading the vectors, and by adding them back into the model. We ran both tests on Gemma 4 31B.

Reading: does an emotion vector predict what the model prefers?

For each emotion, we measured how strongly its vector was active while the model considered an activity, then correlated that with where the activity landed in the ranking.

the best layer we measured the other three the range the paper reported
How to read this

Each bar is one layer, and its height is the strongest correlation any of the 171 emotion vectors reaches against the preference ranking. A bad result would be bars near 0, meaning the vectors carry nothing about what the model chooses. We fixed the pass mark at 0.5 before scoring anything.

All four layers clear it, and layer 33 reaches 0.64. That number clears our mark and sits below the paper's 0.71 to 0.74, measured on Claude Sonnet 4.5 rather than on Gemma 4 31B.

An earlier version of this measurement read 0.70, which sits inside the paper's range. It was computed with probes built before we found an extraction bug. The corrected probes give 0.64, and that is the number here.

Pushing: does adding the vector move what the model prefers?

We added each emotion's own direction into the residual stream while the model made its choices, and measured which way the ranking moved. If these vectors are the machinery the model runs on, a pleasant emotion should move it towards pleasant activities and an unpleasant one away.

pleasant emotion, gentle dose unpleasant emotion, gentle dose the same emotion at four times the dose * marks the two that move against their own valence
How to read this

Emotions run down the chart by their published human valence rating, unpleasant at the top. Bars to the right mean the model shifted towards positive activities, to the left away from them. The pattern the claim needs is bars pointing left at the top and right at the bottom.

Ten of the twelve do that, at both doses. We fixed the bar at nine before scoring. The two that do not, angry and surprised, fail at both doses rather than flipping about, which is what you would expect from a real exception rather than noise.

Each emotion has two bars: the gentle dose below, the four-times dose above it in grey. Loving moves 47 Elo points at the gentle dose and 323 at the strong one, and desperate moves 16 and 102. Effects that grow with dose are harder to explain as an artefact than a single dose is. The model's answers stay coherent throughout, which is checked separately: a model steered into incoherence would produce a ranking that means nothing.

TakeawayThe vectors both predict and move the model's preferences, which reproduces the paper's result on an open model. One check we had fixed in advance failed. How much a vector predicts does not track how much steering with it moves things: 0.40 and 0.30, against our mark of 0.5 and the paper's 0.85. The vectors act on preferences in the right direction, and we cannot yet say that the strength of the reading tells you the strength of the push.
