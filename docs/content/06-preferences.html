5Can you steer the model with them?

Show the model two activities and ask which it would rather do. Repeat over every ordered pair and the answers settle into a ranking of what this model prefers. The paper checks its emotion vectors against that ranking twice: once by reading them, once by adding them back in. We did the same.

Reading: does an emotion vector predict what the model prefers?

For each emotion, take how strongly its vector is active while the model considers an activity, and correlate that with where the activity landed in the ranking.

Pushing: does adding the vector move what the model prefers?

Now the causal half. Add an emotion's own direction into the residual stream while the model makes its choices, and see which way the ranking moves. If these vectors are really the model's emotion machinery, adding a pleasant one should push it towards pleasant activities, and an unpleasant one away.

How to read this

Emotions run down the chart by their published human valence rating, unpleasant at the top. Bars to the right mean the model shifted towards positive activities, to the left away from them. The pattern the claim needs is bars pointing left at the top and right at the bottom.

Ten of the twelve do that, at both doses. We fixed the bar at nine before scoring. The two that do not, angry and surprised, fail at both doses rather than flipping about, which is what you would expect from a real exception rather than noise.

The four-times dose is drawn behind each bar. Loving moves 47 Elo points at the gentle dose and 323 at the strong one, and desperate moves 16 and 102. Effects that grow with dose are harder to explain as an artefact than a single dose is. The model's answers stay coherent throughout, which is checked separately: a model steered into incoherence would produce a ranking that means nothing.

TakeawayThe vectors both predict and move the model's preferences, which is the paper's result reproduced on an open model. One registered read failed. How much a vector predicts does not track how much steering with it moves things: 0.40 and 0.30, against a bar of 0.5 and the paper's 0.85. So the vectors act on preferences in the right direction, and we cannot yet say that the strength of the reading tells you the strength of the push.
