1What we measured, and why it matters

The setup, in one picture

We built each emotion vector by averaging the model's internal state across one emotion's stories, then subtracting the average over all the emotions. To read it back we measure the angle between that vector and the model's state at a given word, which is what a cosine is here. Centered, meaning the mean over the story set is subtracted first, so 0 is the corpus average rather than no emotion.

171 emotions in parts one and two, ~9 stories each; the 12-emotion sets used from part three average 256. Vector quality tracks that count, and section 2 is about how much.

The two models we compare

Gemma 4 31B base and instruction-tuned: the same weights before and after post-training. Running both is a comparison the original paper did not report, and it is where the interesting result came from. Layers are sampled, not swept: twenty across the stack (0 to 57, every third) for the geometry and detection work, six for the trajectories.

The result being replicated

Anthropic reports that emotion vectors arrange on the circumplex. Principal component analysis (PCA) finds the directions along which they differ most, and the top two recover valence and arousal. That matches how humans rate the same words in the NRC VAD lexicon (VAD is valence, arousal, dominance). No human labels enter the fit, so the alignment is the finding.

Why should anyone care whether this works?

  • Readable and steerable in one object. A direction you can read off mid-forward-pass is also a direction you can add back in, which makes an emotion vector an interpretability object and an intervention at once.
  • Models as listeners. People already bring models their worst days, and companies are selling them as companions and therapists. Doing that well means following how someone's state moves across a long conversation, which is what we tested and where it gets shaky. Emotion may not be a major axis of this model's representation at all, and that is the possibility we find most interesting.
  • Internal evidence, for AI welfare. Arguments here lean heavily on what a model says about itself, and reading internal state sidesteps the obvious objection to that. The caveat is load-bearing: what we measured is the model representing the emotions of a story it is reading. That is not evidence about what the model feels.
  • Where its ontology differs from ours. The geometry we recover is the model's, not ours. Where it departs from human VAD ratings, the residual is a map of what is idiosyncratic to the model, and instruction tuning visibly redraws it.
